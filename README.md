# 3D Minecraft Clone

A survival voxel game written in Java on top of [LWJGL 3](https://www.lwjgl.org/) (GLFW + modern OpenGL + OpenAL) — closer to a "survival craft" than to vanilla Minecraft's creative-friendly loop: you gather everything by hand, hunger and lava and falling and drowning can all kill you, and the day/night cycle actually matters. Fully self-contained — no assets to download at runtime. Block textures are generated procedurally into one shared atlas at startup (so chunk meshing can batch many blocks into a single draw call); inventory-only items (tools, food) are also generated procedurally at startup, each into its own small GL texture - see [Textures](#textures) below.

## Features

- **Survival stats**: health, hunger and stamina, all visible as HUD bars. Falling too far, standing in lava, staying underwater too long past your breath limit, and letting hunger hit zero all deal real damage; health quietly regenerates on its own once you're well-fed. Sprinting costs stamina (and a little extra hunger) and locks out until it recovers. Death scatters your inventory on the ground and respawns you at your bed (or world spawn if you haven't slept in one).
- **Tools & mining**: breaking is hold-to-break, not instant - how long depends on the block's hardness and whatever tool you're holding. Each material tier (wood/stone/iron/diamond) is faster than the one below, and six tool kinds each have a specialty: the **pickaxe** (ores - some, in increasing order of rarity, flatly require a minimum tier, so you can't punch out a diamond), the **shovel** (dirt/sand/gravel/snow), the **hammer** (breaks stone and masonry - and mines a 3x3 area of blocks in one swing), the **broadaxe** (the heavy wood-cutter, twice as fast as other tools at wood), the **axe** (leaves and cacti), and the **sword** (combat). Tools wear out with use (higher tiers last much longer) and show a HUD wear bar once damaged; combat is on the roadmap once there's something to fight. While you hold the button the block **cracks through 10 destroy stages** (Minecraft-style overlay) and each punch plays a **hit sound for that block's material** (stone chips, wood thunks, dirt thuds, glass tinkles) - the final break is a louder version of the same material.
- **Foraging**: apples occasionally drop from broken leaves, and rare berry bushes dotted across grassy biomes yield berries when harvested. Select a food item and right-click to eat it and restore hunger — the same button placing blocks uses, since the game treats "use the selected item" as one contextual action.
- **Weather & climate**: the world has its own weather - clear spells, rain, snow, **thunderstorms** and **blizzards** (the severe, heavy forms), plus **fog** - driven by the local climate and the time of year (rainy seasons and humid biomes bring more rain; precipitation falls as snow once it's freezing). Rain pushes humidity up, which feeds further rain, while dry weather drains it back down. Stormy weather **dims the sky**: overcast skies lean grey and drop the ambient light (severe storms nearly black it out, with thunderclouds and fading stars), rain/snow fall as visible particles scaled by how heavy the weather is, and a **thunderstorm's lightning** flashes the sky bright with a rumble to match - and occasionally **strikes the ground**: a jagged bolt crackles down near you, setting the ground (and nearby trees) on fire, damaging mobs and the player within the strike radius, and the fire burns, spreads to wood and leaves, is doused by water, and hurts you and mobs that stand in it. **Winter is dangerous**: out in the open while it snows you get cold - a frost vignette fades in over the screen, your hunger drains faster, and once you run out of food the cold starts eating your health. How cold you feel comes from the **local temperature at your position**, not just the weather: it's colder up a mountain (altitude lapse), and underground is **stable** - caves hover around their biome's annual mean, warmer than a frozen surface in winter and cooler than a hot one in summer, which makes them natural winter shelter. Shelter matters and **enclosed spaces heat up - but only with a heat source**: a sealed room (solid walls on all sides, a roof overhead, solid ground) containing a **fire, torch, lamp, actively-burning furnace or lava** warms the air around you as you stay inside, and **the house keeps that warmth for a while** - it's remembered per area, cools if the space is breached (open a door or break a wall/roof block and the cold gets in; a closed door seals a room, an open one doesn't) or the fire goes out, and slowly ages away even while you're away (~5 minutes), so a fireless house you've abandoned cools back down. A plain sealed room with nothing burning in it stays as cold inside as out. A roof alone still breaks the wind a little, a fire near you warms you almost completely, and **warm armor** - fur especially - cuts the cold exposure down too (see Armor & damage reduction). Falling snow **accumulates on the ground**: exposed surfaces pick up a thin snow coat while it snows, a **blizzard piles the snow deep and fast** (a few layers thick), and the snowline follows the local temperature - it spreads down into temperate biomes in winter and retreats as the weather warms, while naturally snowy biomes keep their snow. The cold also **freezes water over**: exposed lakes, rivers and water sources turn to ice when the temperature drops below freezing and thaw back to water once it warms, so frozen oceans ice over in winter and open back up in summer. Every biome has a baseline temperature and humidity that vary with the season, the time of day and the weather - the `F3` overlay shows the current weather with its strength, the local temperature/humidity and your **cold exposure**, and pressing `H` opens a **weather forecast**: today's hourly forecast plus a rolling 7-day list, imperfect like a real forecast and less reliable the further out it is.
- **A day/night cycle and an in-game calendar**: a 10-minute real-time cycle dims the world and shifts the sky toward dark blue at night, driven by a global ambient-brightness uniform in the chunk shader — darkness is a real, visible signal, not just cosmetic. An in-game **calendar** tracks days, weeks (7 days), months (a configurable 2-4 weeks each), seasons (always 3 months) and years, with named days of the week and months - see the `F3` overlay for the current week/month/season/year. The season feeds back into the cycle: days are long in summer and short in winter as the sunrise/sunset shift through the year. The sky itself is a **procedural skybox**: a gradient that follows the sun's position with matching fog, a sun disc with a warm glow that tracks the cycle, a moon on the opposite side of the sky, a sparse field of stars that fades in at night, and a layer of **drifting fbm clouds** near the horizon that slowly slide across the sky and change cover from one in-game day to the next.
- **Torches**: a real (if simplified) local light source, craftable from a stick + coal. Placed like any other block, a torch keeps the blocks around it lit at a brightness floor that doesn't dim at night, fading out smoothly with distance - see [Notes & Simplifications](#notes--simplifications) for how this differs from true light propagation.
- **Lamps**: a brighter, full-cube light source for lighting a whole room without a forest of torch sticks. A lamp is a solid block with the maximum light level (15, vs a torch's 8), so it pushes back the darkness further and looks like a glowing lit panel. Crafted from a glass block + a torch, placed and broken like any other block.
- **Crafting (Minecraft-style)**: a 3×3 crafting grid - press `E` for the one on the inventory screen, or place a **crafting table** (4 planks) and right-click it for a dedicated crafting station. Click ingredients into the grid, and the output slot shows the result; click it to craft (the result lands on the mouse cursor). **Shaped** recipes match anywhere in the grid and are mirrored (so an axe works with its handle on either side): a log → 4 planks, 2 planks stacked → 4 sticks, a stone ring → furnace, coal over a stick → 4 torches, glass over a torch → lamp, 3 across → 6 slabs, 6 planks → 1 door, 6 planks → 2 trapdoors, 4 planks → 1 crafting table, 8 planks in a ring → 1 chest, 6 planks in two columns → 1 barrel, and the pickaxe/axe/sword/shovel/hammer/broadaxe/hoe shapes in wood/stone/iron/diamond. **Shapeless** recipes ignore arrangement and match on the ingredient combination instead — 1 bone (skeleton loot) → 3 **bone meal**. Adding a recipe is a one-liner in `Crafting.java` (see its javadoc).
- **Farming**: right-click dirt or grass with a **hoe** to till it into farmland (hydrated if water is within 4 blocks). Plant seeds, potatoes or carrots on it; crops grow on wet farmland via random ticks. Skeletons drop bones — crush one (shapeless) into 3 **bone meal** and right-click a crop to jump it ahead, or grass to sprout tall grass and flowers. Farmland sides use the dirt texture, not a grass fringe.
- **Furnace & smelting**: a placeable furnace block (8 stone) that smelts raw ore into the refined ingots/gems iron and diamond tools require. Right-click a placed furnace to open its **furnace GUI**: put ore in the top slot and fuel in the middle, and it smelts over time with a live flame and progress arrow. Fuel: coal (12 items per coal), wood logs/planks (2), sticks (1). See [Furnaces & smelting](#furnaces--smelting).
- **Infinite, persistent voxel world**: chunks (16×128×16) generate on demand from the seed as you explore in any direction with no boundary, streamed in/out around the player and loaded/meshed incrementally so the game never freezes. Memory stays bounded to render distance — only chunks you've actually edited are written to disk, and reloading one restores your edits instead of regenerating pristine terrain, even across a restart.
- **Procedural terrain generation**: layered Perlin/fBm noise for continents and oceans (a low-frequency channel creates real ocean basins and landmasses at a natural sea level), rolling hills and real mountain peaks, a **temperature + moisture biome system** that assigns each column a biome (ocean / frozen ocean / beach / plains / forest / desert / savanna / badlands / jungle / taiga / snowy / tundra / swamp / mushroom field / cherry grove / flower meadow / mountain) and drives its surface blocks, trees and ground cover, winding **rivers** carved as wide shallow channels whose banks sit level with the water, 3D-noise cave systems with lava pooling in the deepest pockets, vanilla coal/iron/gold/diamond, and **GTNH ore veins every 3 chunks** (an ore chunk where `|cx| % 3 == 1` and `|cz| % 3 == 1`, then two empty — one mix per ore chunk, a dense 9-block-tall cuboid). **Small ores** are sparse singles: common metals (copper/tin/zinc/silver/nickel) anywhere for early tools, plus a halo of that mix's small ores around each vein so a cluster of small chalcopyrite actually means a chalcopyrite vein is nearby. Water only forms in bodies that connect to a neighbour, so stray 1-2 block puddles never litter the landscape.
- **Dimensions**: beyond the Overworld there are two more worlds, each with its own generator, sky and save folder (so edits in one dimension never touch another): the **Nether**, a dim red cavern sealed top and bottom by bedrock, made of netherrack with dark pitted soul-sand patches, lava lakes and caves flooded with lava, and glowing glowstone clusters - plus the **End**, a single floating end-stone island ringed by obsidian pillars and scattered islets, adrift in a star-lit void (fall off the edge and you fall into nothing). Travel between them with **portal blocks**: craft an obsidian ring (obsidian itself is made by quenching a lava source with a water source) to make a **Nether portal**, and an obsidian-and-glowstone ring to make an **End portal**. Walk into a portal to teleport - the Overworld↔Nether swap scales coordinates 1:8 Minecraft-style, so a base built at the same spot on each side lines up, and the End always drops you at its island. Arriving in a dimension auto-spawns a matching return portal right where you land, so there's always a way back home; dying in another dimension respawns you back in the Overworld.
- **Biome-varied vegetation**: dense oak forests with fallen logs, mushrooms and tall grass/flowers/berry bushes where it's wet, plains covered in a thick carpet of tall grass with the odd tree, boulder, pumpkin or mushroom, tall jungle trees hung with vines plus bamboo and fallen logs in the hot wet jungle, pink cherry trees in rare cherry groves, carpets of flowers in flower meadows, conical pine trees and mushrooms in cold taiga and on boulder-strewn mountain slopes, cacti and dried dead bushes in deserts, badlands and savannas, dead swamp trees, mushrooms and lily pads in the swamp, red/brown mushrooms on rare mycelium fields, packed-ice spikes and dead bushes on snowy plains and tundra, sandy beaches that hug only the actual coastline, gravel/sand ocean floors with waving seaweed - growing *inside* the water covering it, not replacing it, the way Minecraft's waterlogged plants work (see Notes & Simplifications) - and icy caps on frozen oceans. You always spawn on dry land near the origin, never in the middle of the sea.
- **Generated structures**: rare, biome-gated ruins and buildings appear on flat ground as you explore - hollow sand pyramids with a hidden stone treasure in deserts, snow igloos on snowy plains and tundra, ruined plank cabins in forests/plains/taiga, stilt huts in swamps, and cracked stone pillars on mountains and badlands.
- **Villages**: rare clusters of houses with **pitched stair roofs**, log corners, gravel paths and a stone well — they sit in the grass instead of on a shaved plaza. Plains/taiga get oak, desert/savanna get stone. One village per 32×32 chunk region (hundreds of blocks apart), only in buildable lowland (plains, desert, savanna, taiga, tundra, flower meadow, cherry grove), skipped on steep ground. They can span a couple of chunks; each chunk builds its own slice as you approach. **Doors and trapdoors are functional**: right-click a door to swing it open/closed (it faces the direction it was placed, and breaking one half removes both), and right-click a trapdoor to flip its hatch open so you can drop through.
- **Fast chunk meshing**: per-block face culling (only exposed faces are emitted) with baked fixed-direction shading (top/side/bottom), distance fog, and cross-shaped "billboard" geometry for non-cube decoration (grass/flowers/bushes) with alpha-cutout transparency. Glass and ice are **translucent** - glass renders in a second see-through pass drawn after all opaque geometry, so you can see what's behind it (and glass-to-glass faces are culled). Ice is meshed with the terrain so it writes depth — otherwise water walls show through a frozen lake, and hiding those walls without depth left a hole through the ice.
- **First-person player controller**: WASD walking with gravity and AABB-vs-voxel collision resolved per axis, jumping, stamina-gated sprinting (hold `Left-Ctrl`, or double-tap `W` to latch it on until you let go of `W`), and a creative-only flight mode (`F` to toggle; double-tap `W` also takes off, and once you're already flying, latches on a faster flight speed instead - only `F` lands you, since a double-tap that could switch flight off too meant an accidental double-tap while just moving forward would drop you out of the sky).
- **Swimming**: step into water deep enough that your feet leave the bottom and gravity gives way to buoyancy - `Space` strokes up, `Left Shift` dives down, and letting go of both just sinks you *slowly* (well under a free-fall) rather than dropping you like a stone; still touching the bottom in shallow water just walks you through it at swim speed instead. Sprint-swimming (`Left-Ctrl` or a double-tapped `W`, same as on land) is faster, at the usual stamina cost. Diving in from height cancels most fall damage as a side effect of the same buoyancy - it decelerates you well before a real, damaging impact, the same way water breaks a fall in Minecraft. Flying overrides it entirely (no buoyancy while flying through water), and the existing breath-holding/drowning mechanic (see Player survival below) is unrelated and unaffected - you can still drown treading water at the surface without moving a muscle. The F3 debug overlay shows a `Swimming` line while it's active.
- **Block interaction**: raycast-based block breaking/placing (reach limited to 6 blocks) with a wireframe outline on the targeted block, gated by a real inventory - breaking a block drops it as an item to pick up, placing spends one, and bedrock is unbreakable. Starts empty, so you gather before you build. Survival/adventure breaking is hold-to-break (see Tools & mining below); creative breaks one block per click - each fresh press, not a continuous insta-break while the button stays down, so sweeping the crosshair across blocks with the mouse held doesn't bulldoze a swath of them by accident.
- **Directional block faces**: some blocks have a distinct **front** face that only shows on the side they're facing - a furnace's dark mouth and lintel appear on the front only, while its other three sides are plain stone. When you place a directional block it faces you (like doors and trapdoors already did), so the front is what you're looking at. Facing survives save/reload (each saved chunk now also records block orientations, backward-compatible with older saves) and is the groundwork for any future block with an asymmetric front - an orientation byte plus a `frontTile` in `BlockType` is all a new one needs. A directional block can also have a **lit front**: while its block entity is *active* (see `BlockEntity.isActive()`), the front swaps to a glowing variant and the block emits light - a **burning furnace** shows a bright orange mouth and lights its surroundings like a torch (level 13), turning on/off live as it starts or runs out of fuel. Any future machine (a powered device, an active machine) gets the same behavior by implementing `isActive()`/`activeLightLevel()`.
- **First-person hand**: whatever is in the selected hotbar slot is held in view at the lower-right, Minecraft-style - blocks as a small 3D cube (their own top/side/bottom textures with the same baked directional shading as the world), items (tools, food) as a flat sprite. The hand is glued to the camera as you look around, sways with the walk-bob, and stays fully lit even at night. Breaking a block, placing one, toggling a door/trapdoor, or eating food plays a quick **punch animation** - the held item swings toward the crosshair and back.
- **Flowing fluids**: place a **water source** or **lava source** and it flows like Minecraft - it pours straight down until it lands, then spreads out (water 7 blocks, lava 3) and dries up when the source is removed. Spread is **gradual** - a pool creeps outward one ring every 0.15 real seconds, timed off the wall clock rather than counted in frames (a frame-count throttle would run however fast frames happen to be rendering - uncapped or under a software/headless renderer that isn't actually respecting vsync, that can be far more than 60/second, making "throttled" a no-op), so a placed bucket visibly spreads over a second or two instead of materializing at full extent instantly, while a source's falling column drops at once and stays a **narrow ribbon** (a cell in a vertical column never spreads sideways, so waterfalls don't balloon into fat blobs). Flowing **downhill isn't capped by the spread distance** - every drop resets a cell's distance back to 0, the same as falling straight under a source, so water riding a staircase or slope keeps descending indefinitely instead of drying up a few steps in; only spreading sideways across level ground actually counts against the 7/3-block limit. A resting cell also **prefers any direction that's an immediate drop** over spreading sideways - a real slope keeps a stream narrow the whole way down instead of also flooding out across each row's full width just because it happens to be falling, the same distance-reset that lets it keep descending. Flowing lava burns, flowing water drowns. Oceans/cave lava are static fills that don't move on their own, but breaking a block next to one promotes the exposed boundary into a real source so it starts pouring into the opening, same as a placed bucket would. Flowing surfaces' texture scrolls **in the direction of flow** (away from the source, not always straight down) on top, and straight down on the sides, and a resting cell's depth grades down the farther it is from its source - a full-looking puddle right next to the source, thinning into a shallow sheet by the time it's spread its full distance. Each corner of a fluid block's top surface is averaged with the same-fluid cells sharing that corner (the technique Minecraft's own water uses), so neighboring cells at different heights blend into one continuous sloped surface that meets its neighbors exactly, instead of each cell having an independent flat top that can't represent dipping toward a shorter neighbor on one side while staying full-height on the others at the same time - and each corner's top-face triangulation picks whichever diagonal keeps the surface flattest, instead of a fixed split that can crease visibly across a steep height difference. A falling waterfall/lavafall column stays full height only when the drop is actually 2+ blocks (a real fall). A 1-block step down a slope is a graded stream like the water beside it, so downhill flow no longer goes high-low-high. The column's walls still extend down to meet a shallow pool so you don't see a hole to the dirt, and a source/ocean cell with more water on top does not draw its surface (that's a sheet floating in your face while swimming). Water and lava's texture is soft wavy bands (not flat stripes) with a scatter of bright sparkle pixels for sun-glint, seamless in both directions so the scroll animation wraps with no visible seam. Fluids render in the **translucent pass** (after terrain, no depth write) so a waterfall blends over the cliff behind it instead of punching sky-shaped holes, the air-water surface is two-sided so looking up from underwater isn't an x-ray to the sky, and stream edges map one tile per block of height so a shallow sheet doesn't squash the whole ripple into a smear. Water against **ice** does not emit a wall (you'd see the water's side through the frozen sheet); glass still does, so a window shows the pool. Swimming mobs are drawn before the water pass so the surface covers the submerged body instead of the whole animal sitting on top of the lake.
- **Item drops**: broken blocks drop their item into the world as a small bobbing sprite that falls with gravity and rests on the ground; walk over it to pick it up (items despawn after 5 minutes). Dying scatters your whole inventory around you the same way.
- **Passive mobs**: pigs, cows and sheep wander the surface to make the world feel alive. They spawn on grass near the player (up to a modest cap), idle and stroll around using a small **pathfinding system** - each mob picks a random walkable destination nearby, routes to it with A* over the surface (2D grid of standable columns, allowing single-block steps and planning *around* walls and ledges instead of bumping into them), and follows the waypoints with gravity and AABB-vs-voxel collision like the player's, re-routing if terrain changes under it. Mobs bob while walking, turn to face where they're going, and despawn once they're far away. **Mobs swim too**: step into deep water and a mob floats and paddles at the surface instead of sinking - and if it stays fully submerged (e.g. trapped under a ledge or ceiling) it runs out of breath and **drowns**, taking damage until it dies, exactly like the player's breath. Each mob is rendered as a small **3D voxel animal** - a body, head and four legs built from textured boxes (procedurally painted per-kind skin, self-contained with no image files) using the same meshing and baked directional shading as the world, so they sit in the blocky scene like solid creatures instead of flat sprites; all mobs of a kind batch into one draw call, lit by the same fog/day-night cycle as everything else. Mobs have **health** and can be fought: aim at one and hold **left-click** to hit it (creative kills in one swing; otherwise a sword deals more damage than a bare-handed punch and wears down like any tool). A hit knocks the mob back and panics a passive into fleeing, and killing one drops its raw meat (porkchop/beef/mutton) as edible items - smelt-free, eat them like apples. Transient like dropped items, so a herd never follows you across the map.
- **Hostile mobs** come out at night: zombies and skeletons spawn in the dark around you and hunt you down, pathing around walls and ledges just like the passives. Zombies close in and slug you on contact; skeletons keep their distance and fire arrows (projectiles that fly with gravity, thunk into blocks, and hurt when they hit). Both are killable - zombies drop rotten flesh (barely edible), skeletons drop bones - and both melt away at dawn, so daylight is safe again. In creative you're invulnerable and one-shot everything; spectator is untouched. Hostiles swim and can drown just like the passives, so water is a usable escape route - though an angry zombie that follows you in is a real threat until it surfaces to breathe.
- **Inventory & hotbar (Minecraft-style)**: a 36-slot inventory - 9 hotbar slots plus a 3×9 grid - where items stack up to 64 (tools are unstackable). Press `E` to open it and move items with the mouse: left-click picks up/places a whole stack, right-click does one item, shift-click quick-moves a stack between the hotbar and inventory, and drag spreads a stack across slots. Hovering any item shows a Minecraft-style tooltip with its name (and durability for tools). The in-game hotbar shows the first 9 slots (`1`-`9` / scroll to select). **Creative mode** gets its own inventory screen instead: a tabbed item catalog (Building / Decoration / Materials / Tools / Combat / Armor / Food) over the hotbar, where clicking an item puts a full stack on your cursor (shift-click moves it straight into a hotbar slot) and an "X" slot deletes whatever the cursor holds. Long tabs (Materials, with the GTNH ore set) **scroll** — mouse wheel or the scrollbar on the right — so the grid never runs through the hotbar. A **search box** sits under the tabs: click it, then type to filter every catalog item by name (try `copper` or `diamond pickaxe`). It is not focused when the screen opens, so walking keys don't dump into it, and clicking anywhere else (a tab, an item, the hotbar) unfocuses it. Esc clears the query, then closes.
- **Map (JourneyMap-style)**: chunks in your **render distance** paint onto the map as they load — grass, water, sand, snow, stone, trees, netherrack — with height shading so hills read lighter than valleys, not as a grey grid of ore dots. Turn render distance up in Settings to map more of the world at once. The **mini-map** defaults to the top-right corner, zoomed in to about five chunks around you, tracks your heading with a green arrow and gold **N** / white **S E W** compass letters on dark pills, and marks **discovered** GTNH veins as coloured diamonds. Hold **Alt** in-game to free the cursor: drag the map to move it, drag a corner to resize (it stays square), scroll over it to scale, or press `R` to snap it back to the default corner — size and position save in `settings.txt`. Press `M` for the **full map**: **click and drag** (or WASD / arrow keys) to pan, scroll to zoom on the cursor, `R` to reset. Veins cluster into VisualProspecting-style **ore mix waypoints** (Copper Mix, Magnetite Mix, …) with a diamond, a name label, and a hover popup showing the mix composition and XYZ — but only after you **find** the ore (look at it or mine it, including a surface small-ore). Walking a chunk maps the terrain; it does not cheat the mix onto the map. Older saves fill in terrain as those chunks load again; mixes already recorded on an old map stay.
- **Armor & damage reduction**: four armor slots (helmet, chestplate, leggings, boots) sit in a column just left of the inventory grid, and five material tiers (wood, stone, iron, diamond, **fur**) each reduce incoming damage Minecraft-style - every piece grants armor points that sum into a cap, and damage is scaled by `1 - min(points, 20)/25`, so a full iron set blocks ~68% and a full diamond set the maximum 80%. Click an armor piece in your bag to auto-equip it (or shift-click), click an armor slot to pick it up or swap pieces, and shift-click a slot to unequip back to the bag - mismatched pieces are refused (a helmet can't go in the boots slot). Armor wears down with every hit and breaks like a tool once exhausted, showing a wear bar and tooltip durability; worn armor drops on death alongside the rest of your inventory. Craftable: helmet (5 of a material), chestplate (8), leggings (7), boots (4). Armor also **keeps you warm**: each material has a warmth value (insulating furs hold heat while bare metal and diamond conduct it away), and the warmer your set, the less the winter cold bites you (see Weather & climate). **Fur armor comes in three tiers, harder to get as they get better**: sheep **wool** (common, warm), **wolf pelt** (from the hostile wolves that stalk forests - warmer and tougher), and **polar bear hide** (from the rare, huge bears of the frozen wastes - the warmest and toughest fur of all, a full set shrugs off even a deep blizzard). All three are crafted from the materials the animals drop, so winter survival is a hunt, not a mine.
- **Chests & shared storage**: a placeable **chest** (8 planks in a ring) is a 27-slot (3×9) oak storage block - the lock faces you when you place it, with a distinct lid, sides and underside. Right-click it to open its own container screen stacked above your inventory, and shift-click moves anything between the chest and your bag. Place two chests next to each other on **any horizontal axis** (east-west or north-south) and they **merge into a 54-slot double chest** even if the neighbour was never opened (either half opens the same combined inventory, ordered west-then-north). The pair also **looks like one chest**: half-textures drop the inner frame and put a single lock on the join, the way Minecraft's large chest does. A third chest in a line stays single so pairing is stable. Four chests in a 2×2 square merge into a **108-slot quad chest** (row-major, whichever corner you open) with the same connected look (one south-facing latch across the front row). A cheaper **barrel** (6 planks) is a compact 27-slot stash that never doubles. Contents save with their chunk, and breaking a chest or barrel spills everything inside. Under the hood the whole container system is built on a shared **`StorageContainer`** slot API (`typeOf`/`countOf`/`setSlot`/`add`/`getCount`) that the player inventory, the furnace, the chest and the barrel all implement - with `JoinedStorage` composing any two containers into one view (a double/quad chest today, an item network's storage pool tomorrow) - so a new storage block only needs to implement that interface and the GUI, mouse interaction, quick-move and shift-click logic all work against it with no per-container code.
- **Game modes** (per world, Minecraft-style): picked when you **Create New World**, stored in that world's save, and applied when you load it. **Survival** (the default), **Creative** (instant break, place blocks for free, flight, no damage or hunger, plus a tabbed creative inventory), **Adventure** (no breaking or placing, still vulnerable), and **Spectator** (no-clip flight through the world, no interaction or damage). You can still change the current world's mode from in-game `Esc` → Options → Gameplay — it does not leak into other worlds or the title-screen Options page.
- **Difficulty** (per world, Minecraft-style): **Peaceful** / **Easy** / **Normal** (the default) / **Hard**. Chosen on Create New World and stored with the save; change it later from in-game Options → Gameplay. Peaceful despawns hostiles and stops hunger/thirst drain (health regenerates quickly). Easy hostiles hit half as hard and starvation stops at half health. Hard hostiles hit harder and spawn more often, and starvation can kill you. Title-screen Options has no difficulty row.
- **Slabs (partial-cube blocks)**: stone and planks slabs, the first half-height blocks. A slab occupies only the lower half of its cell - meshed with a top face at half height and a half-height collision box (you can step onto one without jumping), and its block-selection outline shrinks to match. The mesher treats slabs as non-occluding, so full blocks beside/above them render correctly.
- **Stairs & fences (more partial-cube blocks)**: **stairs** (stone and planks) are stepped blocks that face the direction you place them - the low step is in front and the raised step rises away from you, so a run of stairs visibly climbs. Their collision is stepped too (a full-width low step plus a raised back half), and the player auto-steps onto half-block rises while grounded, so you walk up a staircase without jumping. They craft from a 6-material wedge (6 stone/planks → 4 stairs) and mine like their material (hammer for stone, broadaxe for planks). **Fences** are wooden posts with rails that auto-connect to neighbouring fences and solid blocks, crafting from 4 planks + 2 sticks → 3 posts. A fence's collision is a 1.5-block-tall box - taller than the ~1.4-block jump - so neither the player nor mobs can hop over one (see Notes & Simplifications for the thin-box simplification). Both are treated as non-occluding for face culling like slabs.
- **On-screen HUD**: a 9-slot hotbar with block icons batched from the game's shared texture atlas and item icons (food/tools) drawn from their own procedurally generated textures, live stack counts drawn with a tiny procedural pixel font (its own small atlas, no external font/text-rendering library), a highlight border on the selected slot, the **held item's name** centred above the bar (it pops up when you switch slots and fades out after a couple of seconds), a **look-at overlay** at the top of the screen naming the block (or mob) under the crosshair and whether you can mine it with the tool you're holding, health/hunger/stamina bars above the hotbar, transient on-screen messages (death, crafting, tool breakage), an `F3`-toggled debug overlay (FPS/frame time, position, chunk coordinates, facing direction, selected item, biome, loaded/visible chunk counts, entity counts, memory usage, and the targeted block with its break time), a JourneyMap-style mini-map you can drag and resize (see Map), and a full mouse-driven inventory screen (see Inventory & hotbar).
- **Pause & Options**: `Esc` opens a Minecraft-style **Game Menu** (the world pauses): **Back to Game**, **Options...**, and **Save and Quit to Title**. Options is split into tabs — **Video**, **Gameplay**, **Sound**, **Controls** and **Controller** (switch with the `Tab` key or by clicking a tab). Click a row to select it, click a toggle or cycle (Game mode / Difficulty) to change it, and click or drag a slider for the rest (arrows/WASD + `Enter`/`Space`/`Left`/`Right` still work). **Done** or `Esc` returns to the Game Menu. The current value of each setting is always shown next to its control. Options:
  - **Video**:
    - **See-through leaves**: with it on, leaves use an alpha-cutout texture with holes punched through them and stop occluding faces behind them, so you can actually see through a tree canopy (the classic "fast leaves" look); off, they're the default opaque cubes. Toggling it live-rebuilds loaded chunk meshes, so the change streams in while you're looking at it.
    - **Render distance** (3-12): chunk streaming radius, so fewer blocks to draw for speed or more view for looks.
    - **VSync**: cap the framerate to the monitor's refresh or uncap it.
    - **Field of view** (60-110): camera zoom.
    - **Brightness** (50-150%): a world-light multiplier - dim the night or brighten caves.
    - **Clouds** (Off / Light / Normal / Heavy): how much cloud cover the procedural skybox has (Off skips clouds entirely).
    - **Cloud speed** (Slow / Normal / Fast): how quickly the skybox's drifting clouds slide across the sky.
    - **Stars** (ON/OFF): whether the procedural night sky draws its star field (the moon still appears).
    - **Dark GUI** (OFF/ON): the Minecraft-style light or dark theme for the game's menus - the container screens (inventory, crafting table, furnace, chests), the creative catalog, the settings page and the world-selection/world-gen pages all share one procedurally-generated panel + slot art, so the toggle restyles every screen at once. Applies live while the menu is open.
  - **Gameplay**:
    - **Game mode** (Survival / Creative / Adventure / Spectator): the four Minecraft modes, stored on the loaded world — see Game modes. Shown only while a world is open; pick it on Create New World, change it here later.
    - **Difficulty** (Peaceful / Easy / Normal / Hard): stored on the loaded world — see Difficulty. Shown only while a world is open; pick it on Create New World, change it here later.
    - **Mouse sensitivity** (0.03-0.40): mouse-look speed.
    - **Invert mouse Y** (ON/OFF): flip vertical mouse look.
    - **View bobbing** (ON/OFF): the subtle head-bob while walking.
  - **Sound**: a master slider plus one independent slider per sound category, so you can turn down (or mute) just the sounds you want:
    - **Master volume** (0-100%): multiplies every category below - see Sound below. 0% is silent regardless of the category sliders.
    - **Music volume** (0-100%): reserved for a future music system - there's no music yet, but the slider (and its saved settings) are already in place for when there is.
    - **Ambient volume** (0-100%): controls precipitation hiss (rain/snow) and thunder sounds during storms.
    - **Mobs volume** (0-100%): attacking and killing mobs.
    - **Machines volume** (0-100%): block breaking/placing, doors/trapdoors, chest lids, a tool breaking, crafting/smelting results.
    - **Player volume** (0-100%): footsteps, jumping/landing, splashing, eating, taking damage, dying, picking up items.
    - **UI volume** (0-100%): menu/inventory/crafting clicks and opening/closing.
  - **Controls**: the keybind list - every rebindable action (move/jump/sprint, fly, inventory, debug, screenshot) with its current key. Select a keybind row and press `Enter`/`Space` (or click it), then press a key to rebind it - `Esc` cancels. Bindings are saved to `settings.txt`.
  - All of these apply immediately, while the menu is open. The same Options page is reachable from the main menu's **Settings** button before a world starts (minus Game mode and Difficulty, which are world settings).
- **Sound**: footsteps, block breaking/placing, **mining punches that match the block's material**, jumping/landing, splashing in and out of water, eating, taking damage, dying, hitting (and killing) a mob, a tool breaking, picking up an item, doors/trapdoors, **opening and closing a chest** (a wooden lid creak/slam, not the menu beep), opening/closing menus and inventories, and taking a crafting/smelting result all play a sound - positional ones (breaks, footsteps, mining hits, mobs, doors) pan and fade with distance from the camera, the rest (UI, the player's own jump/hurt/death/eat, chest lids) play centered regardless of where you're standing. Block sounds vary by **material** (stone/wood/dirt/gravel/sand/glass/leaves), covering break, place, footsteps *and* the in-progress mining hit from the same handful of categories rather than one bespoke sound per block type. Every sound is synthesized procedurally at startup (see [Textures](#textures)'s "nothing downloaded at runtime" rule, which this follows too - simple tone sweeps and filtered noise bursts rather than recorded/sampled audio) - see [Notes & Simplifications](#notes--simplifications) for what that trades away. Every sound belongs to a category (Mobs, Machines, Player, UI, or Ambient - Music is reserved, see below) mixed against that category's slider *and* the master slider, so the settings menu's **Sound** tab can turn down or mute just mob noises, just UI clicks, etc. without touching everything else.
- **Main menu & world generation**: on launch a main menu (Play / Settings / Quit) appears, Minecraft-style. **Play** opens the world-selection screen listing your saved worlds plus a **Create New World** entry; picking one plays it, and creating one opens the world-generation page where you name the world and set a **seed** (a new random number every time you open the page — you can still type your own), **game mode** (Survival / Creative / Adventure / Spectator), **difficulty** (Peaceful / Easy / Normal / Hard; Normal is the default), world type (Default/Superflat), whether structures (trees, cacti, ...) generate, the sea level, terrain size, and **weeks per month** (2, 3 or 4 - each week is 7 in-game days; 4 is the default) before pressing Done. Each world is saved in its own folder under `saves/` with its settings (including seed, game mode and difficulty), so it reloads on the next launch. **Settings** opens the same Options page as in-game (video, keybinds — not game mode or difficulty) before you even start a world - `Esc` returns to the main menu. In-game **Save and Quit to Title** writes the world and returns to this menu.
- **Multiplayer**: **Multiplayer** on the main menu opens a connect screen - pick a player name, host and port, then **Host & Play** (starts an embedded server in this process and joins it) or **Join Server** to connect to an existing one. A dedicated headless server is available too: `java -jar minecraft-clone.jar --server [port]`. The server is authoritative for the world: every client sees the same terrain (generated from the server's seed - clients regenerate untouched chunks locally and request only player-edited ones over the wire), block breaks/places/re-toggles are validated and broadcast to everyone, mobs are simulated and shared server-side (they chase the nearest player), and other players appear as blocky humanoid figures with their names. Chat with **T** (Enter to send, Esc to cancel) and see join/leave notices. See the section below for what's shared vs. still per-player.
- **Procedural block texture atlas**: grass, dirt, stone, sand, water, wood/planks, leaves, bedrock, snow, gravel, cactus, lava, glass, vanilla ores plus a GTNH mineral set, berry bushes, torches, lamps, and alpha-cutout grass/flower tiles, all generated at runtime into one shared sheet. Ore tiles use a Minecraft-style stone-and-vein overlay (sparse flecks for small ores) with distinct mineral palettes so copper, cobalt, ruby, sulfur and the rest don't collapse into the same grey blob.

## Requirements

- JDK 17+ (only to run a pre-built jar; also need Maven 3.6+ to build from source)
- A GPU with OpenGL 3.3 support (or a software rasterizer like Mesa `llvmpipe`)

## Getting a jar

Every [GitHub Release](../../releases) has `minecraft-clone.jar` attached as a build artifact - just download it and run:

```bash
java -jar minecraft-clone.jar
```

That jar is built automatically by [`.github/workflows/release.yml`](.github/workflows/release.yml) whenever a release is published, so there's no manual packaging step to keep in sync.

## Building & Running from source

```bash
mvn compile exec:java
```

or build a runnable fat jar yourself:

```bash
mvn package
java -jar target/minecraft-clone.jar
```

The packaged jar bundles LWJGL natives for Linux, Windows and macOS (Intel + Apple Silicon), so the one build works on any of them out of the box — just run `java -jar target/minecraft-clone.jar` (JDK 17+ required) wherever you copy it.

### Controller support (Steam Deck and other gamepads)

Plug in (or dock) any GLFW-recognized gamepad - including the Steam Deck's built-in controls - and it works immediately, no setup: keyboard/mouse and controller can both be used interchangeably, even within the same session. The first connected gamepad is polled every frame and folded into the same input the keyboard/mouse drive, so it reaches every screen in the game, not just movement - the inventory, settings, crafting/chest/furnace GUIs all work from a pad too, via a virtual cursor.

## Survival

- **Health** (red bar) drops from fall damage (landing too hard - a couple of blocks is safe), standing in lava, staying underwater past your ~6-second breath limit, or starving. It regenerates slowly on its own whenever hunger is above half and you aren't actively taking damage that same instant. At 0 you die: your inventory is scattered on the ground as item drops and you respawn at your bed (or world spawn if you haven't slept in one, or the bed is gone) with everything reset.
- **Hunger** (orange bar) drains slowly over time (faster while sprinting) and doesn't regenerate on its own - eat food to refill it. Forage apples (a chance from breaking leaves) and berries (harvest a berry bush, found rarely in plains/forest). Select the food in your hotbar and right-click to eat it.
- **Stamina** (yellow bar) drains while sprinting and regenerates while you aren't. Run out and you're locked to walking speed until it recovers.
- **Breath** (cyan bar, Minecraft-bubbles-style) only appears once your head goes underwater - it's meaningless the rest of the time, so it stays hidden rather than sitting at "full" as visual noise. Counts down from 6 seconds; run out and you start taking drowning damage until you surface, which refills it instantly. The F3 debug overlay also shows the exact remaining time.
- **Day/night**: a full cycle is 10 real-time minutes. Night dims the world noticeably - useful to know when it's time to hole up. A **torch** (craft in the `E` grid: coal over a stick → 4 torches, then place it like any block) keeps its immediate surroundings lit regardless of the time of day, so you don't have to hole up in the dark.

## Mining & tools

Breaking a block takes time, not a click - hold the mouse button down and watch **cracks spread across the block** (ten destroy stages, like Minecraft) while each punch plays a hit sound for that block's material. The outline also glows redder as you get closer to breaking it. How long it takes depends on:

- **The block's hardness** - dirt and sand are quick, stone slower, ores slower still.
- **Your tool.** Six kinds, each with a specialty, and every tier (wood → stone → iron → diamond) roughly doubles speed for its matching tool kind: the **pickaxe** (ores), **shovel** (dirt/sand/gravel/snow), **hammer** (stone/masonry, and a single swing clears a 3x3 area of blocks around the target), **broadaxe** (wood - a tier of speed stronger than any other tool), **axe** (leaves/cacti), and **sword** (combat). The wrong tool kind (or bare hands) gets no bonus. Some blocks are gated harder than that: coal needs at least a wood pickaxe, iron needs stone, gold and diamond need iron - anything less and it simply won't break, full stop, no matter how long you hold the button.
- Craft tools in the `E` inventory's crafting grid (or at a crafting table): a pickaxe/broadaxe is 3 of the tool's base material across the top (planks, stone, iron ingot, or diamond gem) + 2 sticks down the middle; an axe is 2 material + 2 sticks, a sword 2 material + 1 stick, a shovel 1 material + 2 sticks, and a hammer 4 material + 1 stick. Craft sticks from planks first (2 planks → 4 sticks). Iron and diamond are **smelted** from their ore first (see Furnaces below), not used raw. See `Mining.java` for the exact hardness/tier table.

**Durability**: every tool wears out - each block it finishes breaking costs it one use, tracked per tool type (wood/stone/iron/diamond each wear down independently, and different kinds like a pickaxe vs. a broadaxe of the same tier don't share wear). Higher tiers last far longer per use (wood ~60 uses, stone ~130, iron ~250, diamond ~1500+), on top of already being faster - so upgrading pays off twice. A hotbar slot shows a thin green→yellow→red wear bar under its icon once a tool has taken any damage; when it finally runs out, that tool is consumed from your inventory (the next one in the stack, if you have any, starts back at full durability). Bare-handed mining never wears anything down, obviously.

## Furnaces & smelting

A **furnace** (craft in the crafting grid: a ring of 8 stone → 1 furnace, then place it like any other block) turns raw ore into the refined ingot/gem you need for iron and diamond tools. Right-click a placed furnace to open its **furnace GUI** - three slots over the player inventory: ore in the top (input) slot, **fuel** in the middle slot, and the smelted result in the output slot. With fuel loaded it smelts **over time** like Minecraft's furnace (a glowing flame between the input and fuel slots shows the burn left, and the ore converts progressively - an arrow fills toward the output as it cooks). Fuel comes in a few strengths - **coal** (the best, from mining coal ore, which drops coal instead of the ore block) smelts 12 items, **wood logs** and **planks** smelt 2, and **sticks** smelt 1. Shift-clicking moves ore into the input, any fuel into the fuel slot, and the output back to your inventory automatically:

- iron ore → iron ingot (iron tools)
- gold ore → gold ingot (no tools yet, but it's there for later)
- diamond ore → diamond (diamond tools)
- coal ore → coal (also mined directly from coal ore)

The furnace works in the background - load it, close the GUI, and come back later. Its contents and smelting progress are saved with its chunk, so a furnace keeps cooking across a restart (the old instant smelt-on-`C` was replaced by this GUI). A broken furnace drops itself and whatever it was smelting or burning.

A **crafting table** (4 planks) is the same idea for crafting: right-click it for a dedicated 3×3 crafting station with its own GUI.

## Saving

The world is saved under `saves/<world-name>/` next to wherever you run the jar from (override with the `MCCLONE_SAVE_DIR` environment variable). Each world's seed, game mode and difficulty are written to that folder's `world.txt` and reused when you load that world; opening Create New World always rolls a fresh random seed instead of reusing the last one. **Your position, look, dimension, inventory, stats and bed spawn** are written to `player.txt` in the same folder, so reloading a world puts you back where you left off instead of at world spawn with empty pockets. Right-click a bed in the overworld to set your respawn point (you can still only *sleep* at night, or anytime in creative). Only chunks you've actually broken/placed blocks in are ever written to disk — untouched terrain is cheap to regenerate deterministically from the seed, which is what keeps disk and memory usage bounded no matter how far you explore. Each saved chunk also carries its **block entities** (persistent per-block state — a furnace's contents and smelting progress today, and any future machine's inventory), length-prefixed by type so a chunk written by a newer build still loads if an entity type has since been removed. Edits autosave every 60 seconds, on **Save and Quit to Title**, and on a clean exit. Graphics settings (render distance, VSync, FOV, sensitivity, see-through leaves) are saved to `settings.txt` in the same directory and restored on the next launch; game mode and difficulty are not, because they belong to the world.

## Multiplayer

Two ways to play with others:

- **Host & Play**: main menu → **Multiplayer** → **Host & Play**. This starts an embedded server on the same machine (default port `25565`, configurable in the Port field) and connects you to it. Anyone on your LAN can then join with **Join Server** using your machine's IP and that port.
- **Dedicated server**: `java -jar minecraft-clone.jar --server [port]` runs a headless authoritative world with no window at all (no OpenGL needed). Clients join it the same way. The server persists edited chunks to `saves/multiplayer_server/`.

The dedicated server reads **`saves/multiplayer_server/server.properties`** (written with defaults on first start) for `port`, `max-players`, `pvp` (whether players can damage each other) and `motd` (a chat line shown to everyone on join). Its console also takes operator commands while running: `list`, `kick <name>`, `ban <name>`, `unban <name>` and `banlist` - bans are persisted to `banned-players.txt` and survive restarts.

The server is authoritative for the world. It uses a fresh random seed each launch (the embedded Host & Play and dedicated server are throwaway worlds; the client always mirrors whatever the server picks). On join you receive the seed + worldgen settings, so your client regenerates untouched terrain locally and only asks the server for chunks a player has edited - keeping bandwidth to the deltas. Everything shared:

- **Terrain and blocks** - a place or break anywhere is validated by the server and broadcast to every client, so everyone sees the same world (including door toggles and directional facing).
- **Dimensions** - the server hosts all three dimensions (Overworld / Nether / End). Walking into a portal block teleports you through the server (Nether coords map 1:8, the End drops you on its central island), a return portal spawns at the landing spot, and everyone sees you switch dimension. Players only see other players in the same dimension, like Minecraft.
- **Time of day** - the server's clock is authoritative and broadcast to everyone, so every client shares the same sky (day/night, sunrise/sunset, and mob spawns all line up).
- **Mobs** - the server simulates every mob (passive and hostile) with its own day/night clock, so animals wander and zombies hunt the *nearest* player; everyone sees the same mobs in the same places. Attacks are sent to the server, which applies damage server-side; when a mob dies, each client drops its loot locally in their own world. Mobs despawn at dawn / far away just like single-player.
- **Players** - every connected player appears as a blocky humanoid figure (procedurally painted skin) that moves smoothly via server-relayed position/look updates at ~20 Hz, with a name above its head. Join/leave and death messages appear on screen.
- **PvP** - you can swing at other players like at mobs: the hit goes through the server (reach and swing-cadence validated), the target's client applies it to their health, and death/respawn flows through the normal server path - including your drops scattering where you fell.
- **Beds & sleep voting** - right-clicking a bed at night sets your spawn and casts a sleep vote; everyone sees a "players in bed x/y" notice, and when *every* connected player is in bed the server skips to morning for all (one player sleeping can't ruin someone's night). If a sleeper disconnects their vote goes with them.
- **Chat** - press **T**, type, Enter to send; the line appears above the hotbar and everyone sees it.
- **Containers** - chests (including doubles and 2x2 quads), barrels, furnaces, the Tinkers' Part Builder / Tool Station **and the Smeltery controller** are server-backed: opening one requests the authoritative contents, and closing the GUI pushes what you left inside to everyone else. Furnaces also smelt on the server, so progress continues while nobody is watching. Two players editing the same container at once is last-close-wins.
- **Smeltery (Tinkers' Construct)** - build a hollow box out of seared bricks (with a Smeltery Controller in one wall) and it comes alive: its controller glows and lights the area; include a **Seared Tank** in the shell as its fuel port. Craft an **iron bucket** (3 ingots in a V), scoop up a lava source with it, then right-click the tank to pour the lava in - each bucket is 100 seconds of melt time (the tank holds up to six). Right-click the controller to open its GUI: input slot, fuel flame that drains as it burns, melt progress arrow, and an output slot. While fueled it melts one item every 8 seconds - raw ore gives **two** ingots (double a furnace), crushed ores give one. Full buckets can also place lava sources in the world. Breaking any shell block pauses everything instantly.
- **Item drops** - broken blocks and mob loot exist once, on the server, so two players can't both loot the same drop: whoever walks over it first gets it (the server validates you're actually near it). Tinkers'-tool drops keep their payload on the client that dropped them instead.
- **Player persistence** - your position, dimension, inventory, armor, stats and bed spawn are snapshotted to the server every few seconds (same format as single-player saves, stored per player name under `players/`), so a disconnect or server restart puts you back where you left off instead of at spawn with empty pockets.

Deliberately **not shared yet**:

- **Health/hunger during play** - your stats are yours while connected (a mob's hits damage only the player it was chasing, via server-relayed packets); they persist via the snapshots above. Dying still respawns you at the overworld spawn - but through the server, so your dimension + position come back consistent for everyone.

A disconnect (server closed, connection lost) returns you to the main menu with a message; Host & Play's embedded server shuts down when you leave.

## Textures

Two different asset strategies, chosen per what actually benefits from each:

- **Blocks** (`TextureAtlas`): generated procedurally into one shared 8×8 tile sheet at startup. Chunk meshing batches many blocks into a single draw call, so sharing one sheet (and one texture bind) across all of them matters for performance.
- **Items** (`ItemTextures`): food, tools, armor and smelted ingots/gems are inventory-only and never batch together the way block faces do, so each is generated procedurally at startup into its own small GL texture instead of a shared sheet - the same self-contained, no-assets approach as blocks, just without the batching that a shared sheet exists for.
- **HUD font** (`FontAtlas` + `TextRenderer`): a full printable-ASCII (32-126) 5x7 pixel font, procedurally generated into its own sheet, is used for every piece of on-screen text: inventory counts, transient messages (death/craft/tool-break), and the `F3` debug overlay. Like the block atlas, it has its own tiny atlas separate from blocks and items since it's neither.
- **GUI art** (`GuiTextures`): the Minecraft-style menus - every container screen (inventory, crafting table, furnace, chests/barrels), the creative catalog, the settings page and the world-selection/world-gen pages - are drawn as a procedurally generated 9-slice panel with recessed slot cells, in a light and a dark theme (see the **Dark GUI** setting). Panels stretch their edges to any screen size while keeping a crisp border; slot cells, counts and tooltips share the same pixel-art look as the rest of the HUD.

All four share one GL upload helper (`GLTexture`) and the same nearest-neighbor filtering, so the blocky pixel-art look is consistent everywhere.

```
├── world/                    # Chunk, World (streaming/meshing), BlockType, Mining, ItemEntity, FluidSim, BlockEntity/BlockEntities (registry), Furnace, RemotePlayer
│   └── gen/                   # TerrainGenerator (noise-based world gen)
├── player/                    # Player controller, PlayerStats, Inventory, Armor, Crafting, CraftingGrid, Smelting, MiningController, ToolDurability
└── util/                      # Noise, AABB, Raycaster, ResourceLoader
src/main/resources/shaders/    # GLSL vertex/fragment shaders (chunk, line, hud)
```

## Notes & Simplifications

This is a compact, from-scratch clone meant to be readable end-to-end, not a feature-complete recreation. Some deliberate simplifications:

- Text rendering now covers the full printable ASCII set via a single 5x7 pixel font (see Textures) - inventory counts, on-screen death/craft/tool-break messages, and the F3 debug overlay (FPS, position, chunk/loaded-vs-visible chunk counts, entity counts, memory, and more) all flow through it. It's still just lines of text, though: there's no layout engine or multi-line UI, so a real recipe book, chat-style log, or death/damage scrolling message list would need building on top of it rather than existing yet.
- Crafting is now a shaped 3×3 grid on the inventory screen (see Features) rather than a flat list of ingredients - press `E`, click items into the grid, and click the output to craft; recipes match on their shape, anywhere in the grid. Pickaxes, axes and swords exist with four material tiers each and now wear out with use (see Mining & tools), but there's no repairing/anvil yet - a worn-out tool is just gone.
- Right-click a bed in the overworld to set your respawn point; at night (or anytime in creative) it also skips to morning. Beds in the Nether or the End refuse to work.
- Stairs and fences render with their own partial-cube meshes (see Features) and collide like their meshes: stairs are two stepped boxes (a full-width low step plus a raised back-half step) that you can walk up without jumping - the player auto-steps onto half-block rises when grounded, same as climbing a real stair; fences are a 1.5-block-tall box (the post is taller than a block), so neither the player nor mobs can jump over one (jump height is ~1.4 blocks). The one simplification: fence collision is a single solid box rather than thin post-plus-rail boxes, so you can't squeeze through a fence's open side at ground level.
- The sky is fully procedural - the gradient, sun/moon discs, stars and clouds are all computed in a single fragment shader from the view ray, so there are no sky textures and the clouds don't cast shadows or move with weather (there's no weather yet).
- Water and lava use translucent, lowered surfaces rather than opaque full cubes, and solid faces toward water are still drawn so you see the sea floor through it instead of x-ray holes. Flowing water/lava spread via a bounded flood-fill recomputed from placed sources each tick (see Flowing fluids) - they pour down, spread across matching static fluid bodies, and flow into freshly-broken blocks, but don't flow upward. Leaves are opaque by default but have a **see-through** mode (settings menu) that switches them to an alpha-cutout texture with holes — oak stays green, cherry stays pink — cutout discards transparent fragments in the shader, so it still needs no sorting and stays single-pass. (Grass/flowers/berry bushes are cross-shaped and alpha-cutout too.) A cell can also hold a **submerged overlay decoration** alongside its fluid - currently just seaweed - the way Minecraft's waterlogged plants sit inside a fluid block rather than replacing it: a separate per-cell layer (persisted alongside the block data), aimed at and broken independently of the water it's growing in, and cleared automatically if that cell's block ever stops being a fluid.
- Day/night affects a single global ambient-brightness multiplier, and caves are exactly as dark as the surface at the same time of day (no separate "underground is always dark" rule). Torches and lamps punch a local hole in that multiplier rather than doing real light propagation: each one bakes a static glow into nearby chunk meshes at mesh-build time, falling off with straight-line distance (Minecraft-style, 1/15 per block) rather than being flood-filled and blocked by walls - so a torch on the other side of a thin wall will still show a faint glow through it. There's also no light bouncing or color tinting, just a brightness floor.
- Chunk generation and meshing both run on the main thread with a per-frame **time** budget (a few milliseconds each, timed off the wall clock rather than a fixed chunk count - the same lesson as the fluid spread pacing above: per-chunk cost varies a lot, especially underground where cave/ore noise sampling dominates, so a fixed count still lets a handful of expensive chunks blow a frame's budget wide open) rather than running on a background thread, so there's no multithreading complexity, at the cost of a brief pause when flying very fast into unloaded terrain. Generation walks nearest-first, and a chunk's **first** mesh jumps the queue ahead of neighbor-seam remeshes — otherwise flying around keeps re-dirtying nearby chunks and a newly generated one stays invisible (you can punch it, it just doesn't draw) until a block update. Underground terrain's cave/ore placement uses a single noise octave rather than the usual three (recalibrated to trigger at close to the original three-octave rate - see `TerrainGenerator`), trading a little fine detail on vein/cave edges for roughly a 3x drop in world-gen's dominant per-block cost. Meshing itself has two more targeted optimizations: each chunk remembers the highest Y it's ever held a block at and skips scanning the empty sky above it (a big win for a superflat world's near-empty columns, a smaller one for the ocean/plains chunks the default generator produces a lot of), and its scratch vertex/index buffers are reused between rebuilds instead of freshly allocated every time - a chunk that gets remeshed repeatedly in quick succession (a fluid pool re-flowing, the "see-through leaves" toggle dirtying every loaded chunk at once) allocates roughly 40% less per rebuild as a result. Rendering itself is frustum-culled - every loaded chunk's bounding box is tested against the camera's view frustum each frame, and only the ones actually on screen get drawn - since most of a high render distance's loaded chunks sit behind or off to the side of the camera at any given moment (measured: with a 70° FOV at render distance 16, roughly 90% of loaded chunks are outside the view and skipped), this cuts the number of chunk draw calls issued per frame by an order of magnitude versus drawing everything loaded regardless of whether the camera can see it.
- World height is capped (128 blocks, same idea as vanilla Minecraft's build limit) — it's the horizontal extent that's unbounded.
- Chunk vertex positions are baked in absolute world-space `float`s rather than being camera-relative, so precision (and therefore visual stability) very gradually degrades if you travel extremely far (hundreds of thousands of blocks) from spawn — not something you'll hit in normal play.
- A crash (as opposed to closing the game normally) can lose up to the last 60 seconds of edits, since that's the autosave interval.
- Sound effects are synthesized (sine tones, filtered noise bursts) rather than sampled/recorded audio - see Sound above for why. That keeps the palette closer to simple retro blips-and-thuds than realistic recordings, and there's no music yet - the **Music** volume slider exists and persists correctly, but has nothing to mix yet; the **Ambient** slider controls precipitation hiss and thunder during storms. Positional sounds attenuate with straight-line distance from the camera (OpenAL's standard distance model) but aren't occlusion-aware, the same simplification torches/lamps make for light - a block break on the other side of a thin wall is still audible at full closeness instead of being muffled. If no audio device is available at all (a machine with no sound hardware, or a headless CI/verification environment - `/dev/snd` doesn't exist there) the game detects that at startup and runs with sound silently, permanently disabled rather than failing to launch.

## Roadmap

This project is being grown incrementally, loosely following [Survivalcraft](https://survivalcraft.net/)'s own real-world update history as a backlog of features to work through - not chasing parity with any specific version, just using it as a source of "what's next" in roughly the order the genre itself matured. Roughly where things stand against that list:

**Done (in some form):** screenshots (well, `F2`)/tools/recipaedia (this README) → **1.1**; snow → **1.2**; furnace + ore smelting + tools → **1.3**; food/eating → **1.5**; buckets/water physics/magma → **1.8** (water/lava flow, no buckets); diamonds/flat-ish terrain → partial **1.12**; saplings-ish (trees regrow via world-gen, not planting) → partial **1.13**; cacti → **1.15**; rain-free weather is still open but thunderstorms/pumpkins are on the list → **1.18** (partial); creative-adjacent options via the hotbar → partial **1.20**; survival/farming (hoe, farmland, wheat/potato/carrot, bone meal) → **1.22**. Also done outside that list: tool **durability** (uses-based wear per tool, with a HUD wear indicator - no repair/anvil yet); **torches** (a real, if distance-based rather than flood-filled, local light source that overrides the night-time dimmer - see Notes & Simplifications); **lamps** (a brighter full-cube light source, max light level, crafted from glass + a torch); a **furnace & smelting** (a real furnace GUI with time-based smelting - see Furnaces & smelting); **slabs** (half-height partial-cube blocks - see Features); **stairs & fences** (stepped, facing stair blocks plus auto-connecting fence posts - see Features); a **shaped crafting grid** (on-screen 3x3 crafting UI, plus a placeable **crafting table** block - see Features); a **container GUI system** (a shared slot model driving the inventory, furnace and crafting-table screens - the extension point for future GUIs like chests or anvils); a **Minecraft-style inventory** (36 slots, stack limits, click/drag/shift-click - see Features); **armor** (four armor slots - helmet/chestplate/leggings/boots - in wood/stone/iron/diamond, with Minecraft-style damage reduction and wear-and-break durability - see Features); **game modes** (Survival/Creative/Adventure/Spectator - see Features); **item drops** (broken blocks and death drops fall to the ground and are picked up - see Features); an on-screen **text renderer** (full-ASCII pixel font powering inventory counts, transient messages and the F3 debug overlay - see Notes & Simplifications for what it doesn't yet do); a **procedural skybox** (gradient sky with sun/moon/stars/clouds, all driven by the day/night cycle - see Features); **dimensions** (a Nether and an End alongside the Overworld, reached through craftable portal blocks - see Features); **sound effects** (synthesized, not sampled - footsteps, breaking/placing, combat, mobs and UI, with a master volume plus a per-category slider each for Music/Ambient/Mobs/Machines/Player/UI - see Sound and Notes & Simplifications); **swimming** (buoyant water physics - stroke up/down or sink slowly, sprint-swim, and dive without fall damage - see Features).

**Not yet, roughly in the order they'd naturally build on what exists:**
- **Boats**, **horse/animal riding**.
- **Electricity**, **temperature effects**.
- A real **recipe book / on-screen UI** now that a text renderer exists - the crafting grid, message logs, death/damage messaging, the world map, and debug info are in-game (see Features), but there's no scrollable/laid-out recipe book beyond the hotbar and crafting grid.
- **Multiplayer depth**: the basics are in (players see each other, a shared authoritative world with synced blocks, mobs, chat, containers *and* item drops - see [Multiplayer](#multiplayer)), but inventories and health/hunger are still per-player rather than shared. Syncing those is the natural next step.

If you've got a specific one of these in mind, just say which and it jumps the queue.

## Testing a PR

Every push to a pull request automatically runs the JUnit 5 unit tests (`src/test/`, covering inventory/stacking, click/drag/craft interactions, game-mode flight rules, crafting recipes and the fluid sim), builds a runnable fat jar, and uploads it as a build **artifact** — open the PR's **Actions** tab and download `minecraft-clone-pr-N.jar` from the latest run. You can also comment `/build` on a PR to trigger a fresh build of that PR's head on demand, and a headless smoke test renders a few frames to a screenshot each run (see `.github/workflows/pr-build.yml`). Download it and run:

```bash
java -jar minecraft-clone-pr-N.jar
```

Run the tests locally with `mvn test`.

## Automated smoke testing

The game supports a headless self-test mode (used to verify rendering in CI/sandboxes without a real display):

```bash
MCCLONE_AUTOTEST=1 MCCLONE_AUTOTEST_FRAMES=90 MCCLONE_AUTOTEST_PATH=out.png \
  xvfb-run -a java -jar target/minecraft-clone.jar
```

Add `MCCLONE_AUTOTEST_TIME=0.5` (0=midnight, 0.5=noon, etc.) to pin the day/night cycle to a specific moment for a reproducible screenshot instead of whatever the default start time renders.

To screenshot one of the menu screens instead of a live world, add `MCCLONE_AUTOTEST_SHOW_MENU=1` together with one of `MCCLONE_AUTOTEST_MENU` (in-game Game Menu), `MCCLONE_AUTOTEST_SETTINGS_TAB=0|1|2|3|4` (in-game Options: Video / Gameplay / Sound / Controls / Controller), `MCCLONE_AUTOTEST_MAINMENU_SETTINGS` (main-menu Options page), `MCCLONE_AUTOTEST_WORLDSELECT` or `MCCLONE_AUTOTEST_WORLDGEN`. `MCCLONE_AUTOTEST_INVENTORY=1` opens the player's own inventory screen with a full iron armor set equipped, so the armor column can be screenshotted. `MCCLONE_AUTOTEST_PARTIAL=stairs` (a climbing run of 4 stone stairs) or `=fence` (a row of 3 connecting fences) lays out partial-cube blocks for screenshots. `MCCLONE_AUTOTEST_MOB_SWIM=1` carves a water pool and drops a pig into it (add `MCCLONE_AUTOTEST_MOB_SWIM_DROWN=1` to cap it with a ceiling so the pig stays submerged and drowns) so mob swimming/drowning can be screenshotted. `MCCLONE_AUTOTEST_SWIM=1` instead submerges the player themself in a deep pool, so the player's own swim physics can be screenshotted with the F3 "Swimming" debug line on.

This runs the given number of frames, saves a screenshot, and exits.
